# Model and feature explanation

**Why ~44% Fearful repeats on unrelated sounds:** The selected SVM uses an RBF kernel. For an input very far from its support vectors, kernel similarities approach zero, so its decision scores become nearly fixed. Calibration then turns those scores into nearly fixed class probabilities. Two different pure tones actually produced identical eight-class scores with Fearful 0.444324897. Stock angry speech-like audio produced Fearful 0.444647515; the app rounds this to 44.5%. This is a real model output but not reliable evidence of true emotion. Distinct files and extracted features can therefore still produce the same score vector. The diagnostic script measures kernel proximity and saves all class scores in `results/diagnostics/`.

An actor-disjoint tuning experiment found a small held-out gain for one C/gamma setting, but the paired actor-bootstrap interval included zero. We retained the original calibrated SVM and its 0.3975 macro F1 baseline. The app checks that `predict()` agrees with the highest `predict_proba()` entry and builds the card and chart from the same probability dictionary. Its optional developer trace shows the exact model file and hash used.

VocalLens also runs a separate pretrained facial-expression model. OpenCV's frontal-face cascade selects the largest face; the grayscale 64×64 crop feeds the ONNX Model Zoo FER+ network. The eight raw outputs are converted with softmax. When a video has several sampled frames, valid face score vectors are averaged. This model was not trained by the team and has no independently measured accuracy in this project. The combined cue conditions each model on seven shared labels and averages the resulting vectors equally. That fusion is experimental, not calibrated or validated. See `src/face.py`, `src/video.py`, `src/fusion.py`, and the project report for details.

Audio is decoded to mono, resampled to 16 kHz, capped at 12 seconds and peak-normalized. Files shorter than 0.4 seconds, silent or invalid are rejected. We summarize 20 MFCC coefficients by mean and standard deviation (40 values). MFCCs describe the spectral shape of speech, a rough representation of vocal timbre. Mean and standard deviation of RMS energy describe loudness and variation. Zero-crossing rate approximates rapid sign changes, which can reflect noisy or unvoiced speech. Spectral centroid describes where high-frequency energy is concentrated. The six summary values plus MFCCs give 46 features.

SVM finds boundaries between emotion classes in scaled feature space. Its score output is calibrated with three-fold cross-validation on training actors only; the resulting label matches the highest model score shown by the app. Random forest averages decisions of many trees and can model nonlinear relationships without feature scaling. Logistic regression is a simple linear baseline. StandardScaler is saved inside the SVM and logistic regression pipelines and fit on training actors only. It is applied automatically to test audio and app input. The forest uses raw features.

Actors 05, 10, 15, 20, 23 and 24 are held out completely. A recording from any of those actors cannot appear in training. All three models use the same split. Macro F1 averages class F1 scores equally and determines the selected model, with accuracy as a tie breaker. The app reads `models/selected.json` and loads that exact saved pipeline. Scores from `predict_proba` are estimates, not guarantees or calibrated measures of a person's emotional state.

On the fixed six-actor test set, calibrated SVM achieved macro F1 **0.3975** and accuracy **0.4417**; logistic regression scored **0.3927** macro F1 and random forest **0.3738**. The selected SVM has the best macro F1 and accuracy, but its margin over logistic regression is only about 0.0048. It recognized just 3 of 24 neutral, 6 of 48 happy, and 8 of 48 sad test clips. These weaknesses matter more than a single aggregate score. The app loads this exact calibrated SVM from `models/svm.joblib` via `models/selected.json`.
